- Waves
  - Waves
    - Wave;;A dynamic, spatio-temporal disturbance that propagates through a continuous medium or field. It involves the collective oscillation of infinitely many coupled local degrees of freedom, without the net transport of mass.
      - basic mathematical description;;$\xi = \xi(x, t)$, where $x$ is the spatial coordinate and $t$ is time.
      - general form for $\xi$;;$\xi(x,t) = f(x \pm vt)$
        - What does the sign of the second term mean?;;Plus means the wave travels to the left and minus means the wave travels to the right.
    - Travelling Wave;;A Wave where energy is transported through a medium without the net transport of matter.
    - Wavenumber;;Spatial frequency of the wave, $k = f(\lambda) = \frac{2\pi}{\lambda}$.
    - Angular Frequency;;Temporal frequency of the wave, $\omega = \frac{2\pi}{T} = vk$.
    - Period;;Time for one complete oscillation at a fixed point, $T = f(\omega) = \frac{2\pi}{\omega}$.
    - Wavelength;;Distance between two consecutive points of the same phase, $\lambda = f(k)= \frac{2\pi}{k}$.
    - Dispersion;;The phenomenon where the shape of a wave changes over time because the Phase Velocity depends on the frequency. (Often neglected in ideal approximations).
    - Phase Velocity;;The speed at which a specific phase (e.g., a crest) of the wave travels. $v_{ph} = \frac{\omega}{k}$.
    - Harmonic Wave;;A wave where the disturbance varies sinusoidally with space and time.
      - general 1D wavefunction (trigonometric version);;$\xi(x, t) = \xi_0 \sin(kx \pm \omega t + \phi_0)$
      - general 1D wavefunction (complex version);;$\xi(x, t) = \xi_0 \exp(i(kx \pm \omega t))$.
    - What is the partial differential equation that describes the propagation of a wave in 1D?;;$$ \frac{\partial^2 \xi}{\partial x^2} - \frac{1}{v^2}\frac{\partial^2 \xi}{\partial t^2} = 0 $$
      - general solution;;$\xi(x,t) = f(x - vt) + g(x + vt)$
    - What is the partial differential equation that describes the propagation of a wave in 3D?;;$$ \nabla^2 \xi - \frac{1}{v^2}\frac{\partial^2 \xi}{\partial t^2} = 0 $$ .
    - Wave Types
      - Transverse Wave;;A wave where the displacement $\xi$ is perpendicular to the direction of propagation $\vec{k}$ (e.g., rope waves, electromagnetic waves).
      - Longitudinal Wave;;A wave where the displacement $\xi$ is parallel to the direction of propagation $\vec{k}$ (e.g., sound waves, spring compression).
    - Mechanical Waves;;Transverse waves propagating along a tensioned string.
      - propagation velocity formula;;$v = \sqrt{\frac{S}{\rho}}$, where $S$ is the tension and $\rho$ is the density.
        - How does Tension affect velocity?;;Higher tension ($S$) increases the restoring force, increasing velocity.
        - How does Density affect velocity?;;Higher density ($\rho$) increases inertia, decreasing velocity.
  - Spatial Wave Distribution & Polarization
    - Plane Wave;;A 3D wave where surfaces of constant phase are infinite planes perpendicular to the propagation vector $\vec{k}$.
      - mathematical form;;$\xi(\vec{r}, t) = A f(\vec{k} \cdot \vec{r} - \omega t)$
    - Spherical Wave;;A wave emitted from a point source where surfaces of constant phase are spheres.
      - mathematical form;;$\xi(r, t) = \frac{A_1}{r} f(kr - \omega t)$, where $r$ represents a distance from the source.
      - How does the amplitude relate to distance?;;Amplitude decays as $\frac{1}{r}$.
      - How does Intensity relate to the distance from the center?;;Inverse Square Law, $I \propto \frac{1}{r^2}$, which ensures total energy conservation as the surface area grows ($4\pi r^2$).
    - Polarization;;Describes the orientation of oscillation for Transverse Waves in the plane perpendicular to propagation.
      - Linear Polarization;;The wave oscillates in a single fixed plane ($\Delta \delta = 0$).
      - Circular Polarization;;The wave vector rotates. Requires superposition of two linearly polarized waves with equal amplitude and a phase difference of $\Delta \delta = \pm \pi/2$.
      - Elliptical Polarization;;The general case of polarization where amplitudes are unequal or phase difference is arbitrary.
  - Wave Energy and Intensity
    - Energy Density;;The energy per unit volume in a wave, consisting of kinetic and potential (elastic) parts.
      - average energy density formula for a Harmonic Wave;;$$ \langle w \rangle = \frac{1}{2} \rho \omega^2 A^2 $$ $\langle w \rangle$ ‒ Average Energy Density $\rho$ ‒ Density of the medium $\omega$ ‒ Angular Frequency $A$ ‒ Amplitude
      - query:Energy Density
    - Energy Flux Density;;A vector quantity describing the rate of energy transfer (power) per unit area.
      - formula;;$$ \vec{S} = w \cdot \vec{v} $$ $\vec{S}$ ‒ Energy Flux Density $w$ ‒ Energy Density $\vec{v}$ ‒ Phase Velocity
      - query:Energy Flux Density
    - Intensity;;The time-average magnitude of the Energy Flux Density.
      - general formula;;$$ I = \langle |\vec{S}| \rangle = \langle w \rangle v $$ $I$ ‒ Intensity $\vec S$ ‒ Energy Flux Density $w$ ‒ Energy Density $v$ ‒ Phase Velocity
  - Wave Superposition and Interference
    - Superposition;;The fundamental property that when two or more waves overlap, the resulting wave function is the algebraic sum of the individual wave functions.
      - What is the mathematical expression of Superposition of two waves?;;$\xi(x, t) = \xi_1(x, t) + \xi_2(x, t)$
    - Path Difference;;The difference in distance traveled by two waves from their respective sources to a common point. It leads to a phase difference of $k\Delta x$ with $\Delta x = x_2 - x_1$.
    - Constructive Interference;;Occurs when two waves meet in phase, reinforcing each other to produce a maximum amplitude.
      - Phase condition: $\frac{1}{2}(\delta + k\Delta x) = n\pi$ for $n = 0, 1, 2, \dots$
    - Destructive Interference;;Occurs when two waves meet in antiphase (opposite phases), canceling each other out so the resulting wave vanishes.
      - Phase condition: $\frac{1}{2}(\delta + k\Delta x) = (n + \frac{1}{2})\pi$ for $n = 0, 1, 2, \dots$
    - Coherence;;The necessary condition for stable interference, requiring that the phase difference between the superposing waves is time-independent.
      - query:Coherence
    - Coherence Length;;The spatial extent over which a wave has a predictable, well-defined phase.
      - formula;;$$ L_c = v \cdot (t_2 - t_1) $$ $v$ ‒ The magnitude of the Phase Velocity. $t_2$ ‒ The moment the wave's phase is suddenly disrupted, hence no more Coherence. $t_1$ ‒ The start of the duration for which the wave is Coherent.
  - Reflection and Transmission
    - What happens when a wave reaches a boundary between two different media?;;A portion of the wave is transmitted into the second medium, and another portion is reflected back into the first medium.
    - What are the Boundary Conditions at the interface of two media used to determine the reflected and transmitted waves?
      - Continuity of amplitude: $\xi_A + \xi_R = \xi_T$
      - Continuity of forces/stresses: $F\frac{\partial \xi_A}{\partial x} + F\frac{\partial \xi_R}{\partial x} = F\frac{\partial \xi_T}{\partial x}$
      - query:What are the Boundary Conditions at the interface of two media used to determine the reflected and transmitted waves?
    - Transmission and Reflection Coefficients
      - Medium Parameter
        - formula
          - $$ \alpha = \frac{k_2 S_2}{k_1 S_1} $$
          - $$ \alpha = \sqrt{\frac{S_2 \rho_2}{S_1 \rho_1}} $$
          - $\alpha$ ‒ Medium parameter $k_1, k_2$ ‒ Wavenumber $S_1, S_2$ ‒ Tension $\rho_1, \rho_2$ ‒ Density of the medium Index $1$ corresponds to the incident medium, index $2$ to the transmitting medium.
      - Formula for the amplitude of the reflected wave;;$$ R = \frac{1 - \alpha}{1 + \alpha} A $$ $R$ ‒ Amplitude of the reflected wave $\alpha$ ‒ Medium Parameter $A$ ‒ Amplitude of the incident wave
      - Formula for the amplitude of the transmitted wave;;$$ T = \frac{2A}{1 + \alpha} $$ $T$ ‒ Amplitude of the transmitted wave $A$ ‒ Amplitude of the incident wave $\alpha$ ‒ Medium Parameter
    - Fixed End Reflection;;Occurs when transitioning to a denser or stiffer medium.
      - What can be said about the value of the Medium Parameter $\alpha$?;;$a > 1$ or $\alpha \to \infty$
      - Does a phase shift occur?;;Yes, the wave is completely reflected with a phase shift of $180^\circ$ ($\pi$).
    - Free End Reflection;;Occurs when transitioning to a lighter or looser medium.
      - What can be said about the value of the Medium Parameter $\alpha$?;;$\alpha < 1$ or $\alpha \to 0$
      - Does a phase shift occur?;;No, the wave is completely reflected without any phase shift.
  - Standing Waves
    - Standing Wave;;A _stationary_ oscillatory pattern formed by the superposition of two _coherent_ waves of the _same frequency_ traveling in opposite directions.
      - What is the difference between Travelling Waves and Standing Waves in terms of energy?;;Unlike traveling waves, standing waves do not transport energy. Instead, energy oscillates locally between pure kinetic energy (concentrated at antinodes) and pure elastic/potential energy (concentrated at nodes).
    - Node;;A point in a Standing Wave where the spatial displacement is always zero.
    - Antinode;;A point in a standing wave where the spatial displacement oscillates with maximum amplitude.
    - Normal Mode;;A specific Standing Wave pattern where all parts of the medium oscillate sinusoidally with the same frequency.
      - key characteristics
        - Formed by specific What are the Boundary Conditions at the interface of two media used to determine the reflected and transmitted waves? (e.g., fixed ends of a string).
        - Features a stationary pattern of Nodes (zero amplitude) and Antinodes (maximum amplitude).
        - Corresponds to one of the system's natural resonant frequencies (harmonics).
  - Doppler Effect and Shock Waves
    - Doppler Effect;;The apparent change in the frequency of a wave caused by relative motion between the source of the wave and the observer.
      - general formula;;$$ \frac{f_O}{f_S} = \frac{u - \vec{v_O}\cdot\hat n}{u - \vec{v_S}\cdot\hat n} $$ $f_O$ ‒ Frequency perceived by the observer $f_S$ ‒ Frequency emitted by the source $u$ ‒ Phase Velocity of the medium $\vec{v_O}$ ‒ Velocity of the observer $\vec{v_S}$ ‒ Velocity of the source $\hat n$ ‒ Propagation direction of the wave
    - Shock Wave;;A disturbance that forms when a source moves faster than the Phase Velocity of the wave in the medium ($v_Q > u$), causing the waves to add up coherently on a cone surface.
      - Mach cone angle formula;;$\vartheta = \arcsin\left(\frac{u}{v_Q}\right)$, where $\vartheta$ is the half opening angle of the Mach cone, $u$ is the phase velocity of the wave in the medium, and $v_Q$ is the velocity of the source.
